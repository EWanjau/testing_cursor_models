    Recommendations

    Fixed to the full Q1 file so they do not drift when filters change. Each item cites a finding.

    1. Review phone quality in Nairobi and Kisumu

      Finding. Orders 1006 and 1022 are the only returns. Both are phones sold to returning customers — 2 of 6 phone orders (33%). Reversed list value is KES 90,000.

      Do. Inspect phone fulfilment and after-sales in those two cities and start recording return reasons. The rest of the range had 0 of 18 orders returned.

    2. Recover Nakuru’s cancelled tablet and list chairs

      Finding. Nakuru is last on revenue (KES 260,000, 16.69%) and AOV (KES 65,000). Order 1014 cancelled KES 50,000 of tablets. No completed tablets and no office chairs in Nakuru.

      Do. Follow up that February cancellation and trial office chairs, the highest-margin SKU (41.67%) elsewhere.

    3. Attach furniture to lift blended margin

      Finding. Furniture margin is 38.84% versus 28.83% on electronics. Chairs lead units (14) and unit margin, but are only 10.78% of revenue.

      Do. Bundle desk + chair in cities that already sell both. Keep electronics listed; this is mix, not a category exit.

    4. Treat laptops as a margin project, not a volume cut

      Finding. Laptops are 33.70% of revenue (KES 525,000) at the thinnest unit margin (26.67%). February’s revenue dip tracks laptop units falling from 3 to 1.

      Do. Work unit cost and attach a chair or desk on laptop tickets. Keep stock: March recovered when laptop units returned to 3.

    5. Copy Mombasa completion and staff a March-like mix

      Finding. Mombasa completed 5 of 5 orders across all five SKUs. March revenue rose 33.48% to KES 614,000; laptops plus desks were KES 400,000 of that month.

      Do. Standardise Mombasa’s pick-and-confirm steps in the cities that returned or cancelled, and align inventory to laptops and desks for high-volume weeks.
